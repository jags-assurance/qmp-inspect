Presenting QMP Inspect to Naresh

A working script and reference for demoing the QMP Inspect prototype — what it is, how to click through it live, and what to say at each screen so the story lands as "this is how QMP actually runs a project," not "here are some screens we mocked up."

Presenter: Harish
Client: Naresh
Product: QMP Inspect for Jags Assurance
Login: Priya Desai · Jags Admin
00

What you're presenting

QMP Inspect is the first module of Jags Assurance's Quality Management Platform (QMP) — the system that runs a construction/infrastructure quality-inspection project end to end: setting up a client and project, drafting the Inspection & Test Plan (ITP), running field inspections across every party in the chain, managing non-conformances, and compiling the final Data Book that gets handed over to the client.

The prototype you're demoing is a fully clickable, stateful simulation — not static mockups. Every action taken as one role (an inspector signing off, a subcontractor submitting a checklist) is immediately visible to every other role, because they all read from the same underlying project state. That's the single most important thing to communicate: this behaves like the real system will, not like a slideshow.

Your one-line pitchOne shared project record. Six parties. Every inspection, sign-off and non-conformance traceable from the first drawing to the handover certificate.

Platform structure

This is the mental model to plant early — before Naresh sees a single inspection screen, he should understand that QMP Inspect is one module inside a bigger platform, and that a project is reached by drilling down through a real hierarchy, not by picking a role off a list.

QMP Orbit→Client→Project→Service (QMP Inspect)
Discipline→Activity→Inspection Instance→360° Record

The Orbit Hub is the platform home screen — every Jags Admin login lands there first. It shows every client, every open action across the business, and which QMP modules are licensed (only QMP Inspect is active today; Surveillance, Audit, Comply and the rest show as "Not licensed" or "Coming soon" — that's the multi-module story told honestly, not oversold).

04

The project lifecycle

Every project moves through the same six stages. The Orbit Hub, the Project Control Centre and the Client's own dashboard all show this exact bar — point that out, it's the same truth rendered for three different audiences.

1
Project Setup
2
ITP Development
3
Execution
4
NCR / Observation
5
Data Book
6
Handover

What happens at each stage

  • ✓
    Setup — parties, disciplines, locations and document numbering configured once per project, reused everywhere.
  • ✓
    ITP Development — the Inspection & Test Plan is drafted from an uploaded specification, then reviewed and signed by all five parties before execution can start.
  • ✓
    Execution — the subcontractor runs the field checklist, the contractor reviews it, the lab uploads test evidence, the client witnesses — then Jags makes the pass/fail call.
  • ✓
    NCR / Observation — a fail becomes a Non-Conformance Report that runs through containment, root cause, corrective action, Jags verification and (for a Major NCR) client acknowledgement before it closes.
  • ✓
    Data Book — the full quality record compiles automatically and is blocked at less than 100% until every ITP, checklist, NCR and certificate is actually in.
  • ✓
    Handover — once the Data Book reads 100%, the Certificate of Handover generates and the client portal unlocks every deliverable for download.
05

Live demo script

Roughly 15–20 minutes end to end. Each act names the seat you're in and the exact click path — read the sayboxes as your talking points, not a script to recite word for word.

ACT 1Sign in & the Orbit Hub~2 min
  1. Open the artifact link. On the sign-in screen, click Priya Desai · Jags Admin.
    Say"Every login only ever sees its own seat — this row is standing in for a real password check today."
  2. You land on the Orbit Hub — the platform home. Point at the top stat row (Active clients, Active projects, Parties across portfolio, Open NCRs) and the QMP ORBIT — Licensed Modules tile row.
    Say"QMP Inspect is one of ten planned modules. It's the only one licensed and live today — the others show honestly as not licensed or coming soon."
  3. Point at My Work and Action Required — explain these are real, computed from live project state, not decoration.
ACT 2Drill into a client and a project~2 min
  1. Click Meridian Energy → Open under Recent Projects. You're now in the Client Workspace.
    Say"This is Meridian's own space — their services, their parties, their documents. Nothing here is visible to Northgate, and vice versa."
  2. Click Open Project Control Centre →. Walk the Project Health bars and the clickable lifecycle stage bar.
    Say"This is the one page a project manager needs — health, team, disciplines, and what's blocking progress, before they ever learn the app's internal page names."
  3. Click into the Civil discipline tile, then into the Foundation F-001 instance row.
    Say"Reinforcement Inspection isn't a one-off — the same activity runs across six real locations. This one is live; the others are the same activity at different foundations."
ACT 3Build & approve the ITP~3 min
  1. Sidebar → Document Vault. Show the uploaded specification, then click Draft ITP from Specification →.
    Say"In production, AI drafts the ITP activities and acceptance criteria straight from this spec and the relevant standards — always reviewed and signed by a named person, never auto-approved."
  2. Sidebar → Approvals. Sign each of the five parties one at a time, watching the sign-off count climb.
    Say"Execution stays locked for everyone — all six seats — until every party has signed. That's not five separate approvals living in five inboxes; it's one gate."
ACT 4Run the inspection across every party~4 min
  1. Switch persona to Subcontractor · Sofia Alvarez. Open the field checklist, answer all five items Yes, attach a photo, sign, submit.
  2. Switch to Contractor · Marcus Tan — his queue already shows Sofia's submission. Approve & forward.
  3. Switch to TPI / Lab · Dr. Amara Rao — upload the measurement report.
  4. Switch to Client · Naveen Iyer — attend & witness the hold point.
    Say"Four different organisations, one shared record. Nobody re-typed anything for the next party to see it."
  5. Switch to Jags Inspector · Ravi Kapoor — all evidence is now in. Click Pass.
ACT 5Show a failure — the NCR lifecycle~3 min
  1. Use Scenario B from Demo Control (see Section 06) to jump straight to a failed inspection with a Major NCR already open, on the Inspector's NCR Register.
  2. Point at the 7-stage stepper: Open → Assigned → Containment → Root Cause → Corrective Action → Jags Verification → Client Ack/Closed.
  3. Switch to Contractor, log Containment, log Root Cause, submit the corrective action.
  4. Switch to Jags Inspector, verify & forward. Switch to Client, acknowledge closure.
    Say"A Major NCR can't quietly disappear — it needs the client's own sign-off to close, and every step is timestamped in the Audit Trail."
ACT 6Data Book & Handover~2 min
  1. Use Scenario E to jump to a fully passed, fully closed, 100% Data Book. As Client, open Data Book — show every category at 100%.
  2. Click Generate Handover Certificate, then Reports & Certificates — every document generated straight from the live record, nothing retyped.
ACT 7Prove tenant isolation~2 min
  1. Back to Priya Desai. Open Northgate Rail's Client Workspace — same screens, completely separate parties, numbering (NGR-JAGS-ITP-001), documents.
  2. Open Users & Settings and read the line: "a subcontractor login can never see another client's records, even by guessing a URL."

08

Anticipated questions

Answer honestly and move on — these are known, deliberate scope boundaries of the demo, not gaps in the plan for the real product.

Q."Why does Northgate look thinner than Meridian?"
Deliberate — it's built to prove tenant isolation (separate parties, numbering, documents), not to duplicate Meridian's full live execution. In production every client gets full depth; the demo only needed to build one all the way through.
Q."Is the AI actually drafting anything, or is that just a button?"
In this prototype, cosmetic — it demonstrates the intended flow (spec in, draft ITP with suggested activities and intervention points out, human reviews and accepts) without a live model behind it yet. Always framed as assistive, never as the approver of record.
Q."Can a subcontractor accidentally see another client's project?"
No — access is enforced by row-level security in the real system, not by hiding menu items. Point at the note on the Users & Settings screen.
Q."What happens offline, on site with no signal?"
Open item for the build phase — the field checklist is designed for tablet-first capture; exact offline behaviour is one of the scoping questions still to confirm with you before development starts.
Q."How long will the real build take?"
Have your effort estimate ready separately — don't quote a number live unless asked directly, and frame it as "full-time weeks for QMP Inspect alone," not the whole ten-module platform.
